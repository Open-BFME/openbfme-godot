# Open BFME — beta v0.2.1

The first build published through `dist\`. Everything below is player-facing:
what you will see differently when you start this build, in plain words.

## How to start it — read this first

**Double-click `run-with-log.bat`.** Not `OpenBFME.exe`.

This build does not find its own content packs; the .bat points it at the ones
in this folder. Starting the exe directly gets you a game that loads whatever
old content is lying around in your user profile, or none — and it will not
look like an error, it will look like the game lost half its factions. If you
have the release-candidate folder, `Play-With-Launcher.bat` works too.

Making the exe self-sufficient is a known follow-up; until it lands, the .bat
is the supported way in and `BUILD-INFO.txt` records that this build was tested
both ways.

## Create-a-Hero is a complete loop

You can build a hero, take him into a skirmish, and keep him.

- **Create → customise → play** works end to end. The hero you build in the
  creation screen is the hero that walks onto the map.
- **Your retail heroes come across.** Point the game at a `.cah` file from a
  real BFME2/RotWK profile and it reads it — name, class, powers, colours.
  Heroes made years ago in the retail game are usable here without redoing them.
- **Garments are ordered the way retail orders them.** Helmets, cloaks and
  armour layer in the same sequence the original game used, so a hero looks
  like the hero you designed instead of a pile of overlapping pieces.

## The Ring

- **Ring heroes exist.** Holding the Ring long enough transforms a hero, per
  faction, using retail's own rules for who is eligible and what they become.
- **Gollum is in.** He drops the Ring where retail drops it, and he looks and
  moves like Gollum rather than a placeholder.

## Maps and the battlefield

- **Castle maps are visible.** They appear in the map list with their real
  names and previews. (See Known gaps — they are visible, not yet launchable.)
- **Fog of war.** The map is dark until you scout it, and it stays remembered
  once you have. Enemies you cannot see are not drawn.
- **The map edge behaves.** Scrolling to the border and zooming in and out no
  longer punches holes in the fog or shows you the void past the terrain.

## What you can see and do in a battle

- **Health bars on units and on fortresses.** You can tell at a glance what is
  hurt, including your own walls and gate.
- **A command wheel.** Right-hand orders for the selected unit are on a wheel
  instead of buried, so stances, formations and abilities are one gesture away.
- **Arrows look like their faction — in the code, probably not yet in this
  build.** Elven, dwarven, Mordor and Isengard archers fire their own arrows
  instead of a shared generic one. The content packs bundled here were built
  before that work landed, so you will most likely still see the shared arrow
  until the next republish. Check, but do not report it.
- **Fortress artillery fires.** Fortress-mounted weapons actually shoot at what
  is attacking you instead of standing decoratively still.

## Sound

- **EVA groundwork.** The announcer system that says "you are under attack" and
  its siblings is wired up, ready for the voice lines to land on top of it.
- **Mordor rams talk.** The siege ram crews have their retail voices back.

## Known gaps — read this before reporting a bug

These are known and are not worth a report:

- **Castle maps will not start.** They appear in the list and then refuse to
  launch. The walls and gates they need are still being built; picking one is
  expected to fail until that lands.
- **Created heroes are silent.** A hero you make has no voice lines yet. The
  voice packs are a separate piece of work.
- **Arrows still look shared.** Expected: the packs in this build predate the
  arrow work. The fix is in the code and arrives with the next republish.
- **The exe is not self-sufficient.** Start with `run-with-log.bat`; see the
  top of this file.
- **EVA is mostly quiet.** The system is in; most of the actual announcements
  are not recorded into the packs yet.
- **War of the Ring** ships only if its converted data was present when this
  build was made. `BUILD-INFO.txt` in the build folder says exactly which parts
  are there and what you lose without them — it is generated, not guessed.

## Where the numbers come from

`BUILD-INFO.txt` and `BUILD-INFO.json` next to the game name the commit, the
branch, whether the working tree was dirty, and every content pack with its
hash. If a feature you expect is missing, check those first: a build is only
ever as new as the commit it was made from.
